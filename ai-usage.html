AI Usage Principles

How Not to Misuse AI
A practical guide for people using AI in professional or creative work

In each principle I’ll use a simple example from building a web app with AI. These aren’t hard rules. They’re practical guardrails that keep the work real and useful.

⚠️ Principle 1: Know Enough to Spot Wrong

If you can’t tell whether the AI is right or wrong, don’t use it for that task. AI can sound certain while being off. Misuse shows up when people ask for answers in domains they don’t understand.

Trust requires judgment. If you can’t recognize an error, you’re not ready to use the tool for that task.

Example:

If you ask AI to build a web app and it picks Django instead of something like React + Node, would you even know the difference? If not, you might be building the wrong kind of application.

🛠️ Principle 2: Experiment Freely, Ship Carefully

Play with AI. Try things. Break things. That’s how you learn. When you ship to production, especially if others will rely on it, you own the quality.

Curiosity is encouraged. Irresponsibility isn’t.

Example:

Using AI to generate a login form is fine. Copy-pasting that code into a live app without understanding authentication or password hashing is a risk multiplier for your team and users.

🧠 Principle 3: AI Isn’t a Brain. You Are.

AI is pattern completion at scale. It doesn’t reason like a human. You bring strategy, ethics, and goals. It’ll follow your direction, even if your direction is flawed.

You can’t delegate decisions to a tool that doesn’t understand context.

Example:

If you say “build me a booking system,” AI will start spitting out components. Only you can define what it needs: time zones, cancellation rules, capacity, audit trails. That’s on you.

🧭 Principle 4: Clarity In, Clarity Out

Results track your ask. AI mirrors your input. Give context, constraints, and acceptance criteria if you want dependable output.

Be specific. Be intentional. The prompt is the project.

Example:

“Build me a budgeting app” gets you something vague. “Build a React app that lets users input income and expenses, save them locally, and export to CSV” gets you something usable.

⏳ Principle 5: Don’t Skip the Thinking

AI moves fast. It won’t think for you. If you don’t define goals, architecture, and edge cases, you’re just speeding into confusion.

AI can amplify your vision, but it can’t replace it.

Example:

You ask AI for a dashboard. It returns a beautiful UI. There’s no data validation, no state management, no business logic. You never asked for those because you skipped the thinking.

🤝 Principle 6: Collaborate With It, Don’t Delegate Blindly

Treat AI like a fast partner. Ask questions, test ideas, and iterate. Collaboration beats one-shot delegation.

Use it as a sharp assistant, not an expert builder.

Example:

Instead of “build this whole app,” try “here’s my file structure, help me design a signup flow that stores user info in localStorage.” That’s collaboration, and it works better.

Final Word

This isn’t about rules. It’s about stewardship.
We’re the ones shaping how AI shows up in the world. Let’s do it with clarity, curiosity, and responsibility.
